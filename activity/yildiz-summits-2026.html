Yıldız Summits

5 Graduates, 3 Peaks & Skinny Dip · 3–7 July 2026 · Aladağlar

🏕
Summits on this trip
▲ Yıldızbaşı 3,685 m ▲ Yıldızbatı 3,700+ m ▲ Emler 3,723 m

Getting There & Back

Departed Dudullu (Ataşehir) at 21:00 on Friday 3 July. The bus had a breakdown in Gebze (Aydoğanlar lines) causing a delay of at least two hours. Arrived Niğde bus station at 09:00. From there, Fikret's pickup truck took the group to Çamardı village in approximately 40 minutes. Ulvi abi then drove the group to the trailhead (30 minutes). A three-hour walk brought the team to Oba Yeri camp by early afternoon.

Return on 6 July: walked out in 2 hours 15 minutes, meeting Ulvi abi at the agreed point at exactly 14:00. Dropped at Çamardı at ~15:00. Drove back via Fikret's village, ate in town, and caught the 20:45 / 21:00 buses back to Istanbul.

Summit Day — 4 July

Woke at 04:00. Everything had been prepared the night before — food eaten at camp the evening before to avoid a heavy breakfast — so the group set off almost immediately after waking. A few minutes were lost searching for Efsane's missing trekking pole (it turned out to be sitting next to another hiker's gear at camp; nobody recognised it as theirs until the return). One pole was shared: the person carrying the shared rucksack took it, alternating with their partner — this caused no problems.

The ascent was very steep throughout. Both Yıldızbaşı and Yıldızbatı were reached 1 hour 15 minutes ahead of the planned schedule. The first summit was cold and very windy. Slab terrain was noticeably preferable to loose scree — less tiring and more stable, though a sheer drop sits behind several of the slab steps and must be treated with care. Changing wet base layers at the first summit is strongly recommended (the second climber in the group who did this found the wind far more manageable than those who did not).

The traverse from Yıldızbaşı to Yıldızbatı was easier in character than the initial ascent. Morale was high. A rest of approximately one hour was taken at Yıldızbatı — the group ate, recovered, and looked ahead at the second half of the route. Strong wind made it cold and cut the rest short.

Descent & Valley Traverse

The descent from Yıldızbatı proved to be the most demanding section of the day. The trail was buried under unmelted snowfields, forcing continuous improvised traversing on steep terrain. At one point a snow-covered slab traverse had a cliff drop directly behind it — extreme care was needed.

Part way down, the group split into two: Efsane and Arca took a lower line and reached the snow ramp before the others; Alp, Fikret, and Beril descended higher and reached the alpine plateau from a different snow section. Both groups remained within sight of each other throughout. Snow depth was sufficient for glissading; thickness should be checked before committing to a slide.

The plateau was mostly flat with scattered snow. The group navigated around obstacles to reach Dipsiz Göl (Bottomless Lake). Those who wanted swam; others rested on the shore. A 30-minute walk from the lake returned the group to Oba Yeri campsite.

Camp & Day Three

Back at camp: ate dinner, played DUO cards, rested at length. Good group time after a long summit day. Prepared for the next day's objective before sleeping at sunset.

The planned third summit day (additional peak) did not happen — no one was able to get up. The group woke at 10:00, had a light breakfast of quick soups, broke down camp and began the walk out. Reached the meeting point exactly on schedule at 14:00.

Hazards & Notes for Future Trips

  • Snow had not melted in early July — trails were covered and icy in many places.
  • Rock is extremely loose on the upper ridge — never pull on a hold without testing it first.
  • Several traverse sections are exposed with a drop-off directly behind the path.
  • The camp stream at Oba Yeri is present in summer but dries up in some years — check before departure.
  • Phone signal works on the ridge (not Vodafone). Garmin GPS watch was erratic — use phone map as primary navigation.
  • Bring an extra T-shirt. Swap the wet base layer at the first summit before the wind gets to it.
  • Mark where group gear (poles, shared packs) is left at camp.
  • If visiting in winter or early season, altitude sickness is possible at Oba Yeri. Allow acclimatisation time.
  • No refill needed for the descent — water points exist on the trail down.
