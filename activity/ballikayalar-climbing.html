1 July 2026 Rappel Station Training Report

Main objective: To establish a safe station for rappelling, perform the rappel, and dismantle the station.

Critical Points

  • Two anchor points must always be used in order to maintain safety at the highest level.
  • In systems where a prusik (rope tied with a double fisherman’s knot) or a looped rope (such as webbing) is used from two anchor points, a knot or loop must be made so that both anchors function (are redundant).
  • The two ends of the rope to be descended must always be joined with an end-of-rope knot.
  • The most experienced person in the group must be the leader and descend last (a leader must be chosen even if experience levels are equal).

Types of Stations

  • In places like Ballıkayalar where there is a ready-made station designed for rappelling with two bolt anchor points, the rope to be descended can be used by passing it directly through both points. In this way, the rappel can be performed without leaving equipment above.

Natural Stations

  • Hourglass: A natural formation in the rock with two holes through which a rope can be passed from its entrance and exit.
  • Tree root: An exceptional example where a single station may be safe. It must be ensured that the plant is fixed and that the rope passed around it will not come off the plant (that is, it must be tall enough).

Artificial Stations

  • Piton: It is established by hammering into the rock two metal pieces, with a ring at one end and a relatively pointed end at the other. Once hammered in, these serve as anchor points. CAUTION: A piton is not hammered just anywhere. It must be hammered into a rock crack, and before hammering it in, the rock must be struck and the resulting sound considered; it must not be hammered into a hollow place. Most of the time, someone has previously hammered a piton at the place where the rappel will be made.

Steps After Obtaining Two Anchor Points

  1. Before the rappel, carabiners are attached to the two anchor points.

2. Equalizing and Securing the Station

  • Pass the Webbing: Pass a wide webbing sling through both carabiners.
  • Create a Master Point: Pull the lower middle of the webbing downward (in the direction of descent). Catch both strands and tie a double-strand figure-eight knot. This gives you a fixed Master Point that distributes the load equally between the two points. OR create a loop with one of the strands and bring it alongside the other strand.
  • Attach a Carabiner: Place one locking carabiner in this master point OR inside the loop.

2. Preparing the Rappel Rope

  • Tie: Always join both ends of the rope with a safety knot (overhand knot).
  • Centre: Find the exact middle of the rappel rope, pass it through the locking carabiner at the station, and lock the carabiner.
  • Throw: Throw the rope downward along the descent route in several small bundles (not all at once). Make sure the ropes reach the bottom.

3. Attaching the Descender

  • Attach the ATC: Put on your harness and place the descender (ATC) on the locking carabiner at the main belay loop.
  • Pass the Ropes: Pass both rappel ropes through the ATC and the carabiner, then lock the carabiner.

4. Setting Up the Prusik (Autoblock) System

  • Carabiner on the Leg Loop: Attach a locking carabiner to the leg loop of the harness.
  • Tie a Prusik Knot: Take your accessory cord. Form a classic Prusik Knot around both strands of the rappel rope by passing it through at least three times.
  • Attach to the Carabiner: Clip the end of the Prusik knot to this separate carabiner on the leg loop and lock it. (This way, the ATC remains above and the Prusik below at the leg loop; they do not collide with each other.)
  • Check: Pull the Prusik downward by hand to make sure it grips the rope firmly and slides easily when pushed upward.

5. Descent

  • Final Check: Visually and manually check the station knot, webbings, all locking carabiners, and harness buckles.
  • Descent: Put a little body weight onto the system and test that it works. Loosen the Prusik knot with one hand and control the lower rope with the other hand to begin the descent safely.

The Last Person to Descend and Collecting the Quickdraws

We cannot leave the quickdraws above because they are expensive. Instead, the leader—the last person to descend—removes the quickdraws and establishes a station with Prusik cord—because it is inexpensive and can be left above. Two Prusik cords are passed through these two anchor points and loops are created by tying fisherman’s knots. The rappel rope is then passed through these Prusiks. After the leader completes the descent, they untie the knots at the end of the rappel rope and pull the rope downward.
