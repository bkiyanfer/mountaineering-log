TRANSPORTATION INFORMATION:

The summit activity began on Friday evening, 27 February, when our Asya Mountaineering club vehicle departed from in front of Alfemo in Bakırköy–İncirli at 23:00. Apart from overnight comfort stops for soup, we continued without stopping. After breakfast at a rest facility in Aksaray at around 08:00 on the morning of 28 February, we reached the campsite.

The return journey began at 18:30 on 1 March, after the summit, from the campsite at 2,000 m on Hasan Dağı. Again stopping only for comfort breaks, we arrived in Istanbul on Monday morning, 2 March.

Activity Flow:

When we reached the campsite, we first collected our bags, which had been brought up by pickup truck, took out our tents, and selected a suitable area. Because of the deep snow, we had to compact it thoroughly before pitching the tents. Once the tents were up, we melted snow, boiled pasta, and mixed it with kavurma. While Behzad Abi made eggs with dates for us, we also began preparing enough toasted sandwiches for everyone for the summit. After dinner and the summit preparations, we went into our tents to sleep as the sun set.

The temperature dropped seriously during the night. I (Beril) woke up from the cold several times and eventually asked Efsane for some of her heat pads. The ones I had bought from Decathlon did not work, but Efsane’s from ŞOK worked very well and got me through the night.

We woke at around 03:00, made our final summit checks, and began walking at 04:15 together with Murat Hoca’s group. We gained elevation quickly with trekking poles, taking only short breaks, but our pace dropped significantly when we caught up with the group ahead. We realised that we had been able to move so fast because they had compacted the snow and made a trail. The slower pace caused us to become seriously cold, and we had to put on our down jackets.

At one point the snow deepened so much that we had to put away the poles, switch to ice axes, and fit our crampons. At this point the sole of Umut’s boot came apart (the boot had previously been repaired, but apparently not properly), yet he wanted to continue rather than turn back. While putting on her crampons, Efsane’s outer glove and crampon bag also slid downhill. She managed to go back and retrieve the crampon bag, but we could not find the glove. Heavy fog and precipitation gave us very little visibility where we stood, and these incidents made us realise how steep the slope was.

Once we emerged from the foggy section, we relaxed a little: the sunlight could reach us and warm us, and seeing the summit motivated us. Murat Hoca said the final 350 m would take at least another two or three hours, and it did. Because of the depth of the snow and the angle of the mountain’s upper slopes, we climbed slowly with our ice axes and reached the summit. We were above the clouds. We took out the toasted sandwiches prepared the day before, but, as expected, they were frozen. After snacking on the other food we had brought—fruit leather, nuts, halva, jelly sweets, and protein bars—we stayed at the summit for only about 20 minutes and began the descent. The descent was easier than the climb. Immediately below the summit, we down-climbed with the support of our ice axes and crampons. With so much snow, we descended without overloading our knees, using the ice axe at first and switching to trekking poles later.

When we reached camp, we had to pack quickly and board the vehicle. We were ready in about 1.5 hours and took our seats for the 18:30 departure.

DETAILS:

At the Campsite:

  • You can melt snow for water.
  • For those who do not wish to camp, a hotel normally operates right beside the site, but it was closed for maintenance when we visited.
  • Because the campsite is at altitude, mountain sickness may occur.
  • There is internet reception on the nearby higher ground.
  • You should definitely have two good sets of base layers, a shell jacket, a down jacket, and a fleece.
  • You can bring heat pads to stay warm at night. The ones sold at ŞOK work; the Decathlon ones did not warm up.

During the Activity:

  • There were deep layers of snow all along the summit route; it is important to go with someone who knows the trail.
  • We encountered dense fog and precipitation while ascending, so suitable clothing is essential.
  • A CamelBak is definitely unsuitable for a winter summit because the water becomes too cold to drink. The best option is to put water boiled the night before or prepared in the morning into a vacuum flask and drink it warm throughout the summit day.
  • It is important to eat the toasted sandwiches at camp. They freeze on the summit route.
  • Carry painkillers; cold and altitude can cause dizziness and nausea.

Return:

  • We kept our crampons on until the very end.
